# Spec: Products

Part of the [spec](../spec.md), which has the summary, the terms, the quality rules and the index of every area file.

Products are the team's price list: what the business sells and what it charges for each one. Later changes will use them on deals, quotes and invoices. For now they stand alone, and nothing else in the app links to them.

## Products

- **G1.** Products is the sixth section in the top bar, after Pipeline, at `/products`. Each product has its own page, at `/products/:id` (N2). A product that doesn't exist shows "This product no longer exists" with a link back to Products (N4).
- **G2.** The Products table has the columns Name, Description and Unit price, sorted by name by default. It follows the other tables: search, sortable headings, an Add button, 50 rows a page, its view kept in the address, and the empty and no-match messages (T1 to T5, T7). Each row has Edit and Archive, or Restore for an archived product, in place of Delete. Search matches the name and the description. Unit price sorts by amount. A long description is cut to one line with an ellipsis, and shows whole on the product's page.
- **G3.** Above the table, a filter shows Active, Archived or All, each with its count, like the Contacts status filter (T6). It starts on Active, so archived products are hidden until someone chooses to see them. The choice stays in the address and works together with search. Clearing a search with no matches also puts the filter back to Active, as T7 does on Contacts. In the All view, an archived product has an Archived chip after its name.
- **G4.** Add and Edit open the same form in a dialog (F1), with Name and Unit price, both required, and Description, optional, in a box that takes several lines. A save shows "Product saved.", and a save that fails keeps the dialog open with what was typed (F10). A new product is active. Whether a product is active isn't a field in the form: Archive and Restore change it.
- **G5.** The unit price is typed in dollars and cents, in a box with `$` shown before it. It takes `49.50`, `49.5`, `49`, `1200` and `1,200.00`, with or without a `$`, and spaces at either end are ignored. Zero is allowed, for something given away, and the most is $1,000,000.00. A blank box says "Enter a price". More than two decimal places, a minus sign or anything that isn't a number says "Enter a price in dollars and cents, such as 49.50". Over the most, it says "Enter a price of $1,000,000.00 or less". The form turns the text into whole cents by reading the digits, never through a floating-point number, so `0.29` is 29 cents and not 28. Editing a product shows its saved price as `49.50`.
- **G6.** Prices always show in dollars and cents, with the currency, a comma between thousands and two decimal places, as in `$49.50`, `$1,200.00` and `$0.00`. That holds everywhere a price appears: the table, the product's page and the search results. Prices are in JetBrains Mono, right-aligned in the table so the decimal points line up.
- **G7.** A product's name is required, and spaces at either end are dropped before it's saved. Two products can't share a name, ignoring case in any language, so "Training day" and "TRAINING DAY" clash, and so do "Straße" and "STRASSE". The rule covers archived products too, so an archived product keeps its name. A product can always keep its own name, or change only its case, such as "training day" to "Training day". A clash with an active product says "There's already a product called Training day." beside the name. A clash with an archived one says "An archived product is already called Training day. Restore it or choose another name." The backend makes the check, so it holds through the page and the API alike, and the database refuses a second copy of the name too, so two saves at the same moment can't both get through.
- **G8.** Archive and Restore are on every row and on the product's page. They don't ask first, because each undoes the other. Each happens once, however quickly its button is pressed, and if it fails the product stays as it was and a message says so. After archiving, the message reads "Training day archived." and the product leaves the Active view and the search results, but keeps its name, price and description, and who added and last changed it. Restore puts it back as it was, with "Training day restored." Archiving and restoring count as changes to the product (U10). An archived product can still be edited.
- **G9.** Products can't be deleted. There's no Delete button and no delete route in the API. In the local copy, `npm run seed` and `npm run empty` replace or remove them with every other record (C4).
- **G10.** A product's page shows its name as the page title, an Archived chip beside it if it's archived, its unit price, its description with its line breaks kept, or "None" if it has none, and who added it and who last changed it, as on the other record pages (R6). It has Edit, and Archive or Restore. It has no timeline, because products have no activities. After an edit, archive or restore, it stays on the page and shows the change.
- **G11.** The top bar's search finds active products by name, in a Products group after Deals ([K1, K4](search.md#search)). Archived products are found from the Products table, with the filter on Archived or All.

## Screens

### Products table

Follows the [table screen](records.md#tables-1): the page title with Add product beside it, then the search box and the Active, Archived and All filter on one line, built like the Contacts status filter, then the table on a panel. Description is in muted ink. Unit price is the last data column before the row's buttons, right-aligned. Edit and Archive, or Restore, are icon buttons at the end of each row, always visible, with labels for screen readers such as "Archive Training day". Archive uses lucide-react's `Archive` icon and Restore its `ArchiveRestore` icon, both in ink. Neither is red, because neither loses anything.

The Archived chip is a small chip in the page color with muted ink text, in the label style. It reads as quieter than the stage and status chips, because an archived product is out of use rather than in a state of its own.

### Product page

The same header as the other [record pages](records.md#record-pages-1): the name as the page title, the Archived chip beside it when there is one, and Edit and Archive, or Restore, in place of Edit and Delete. Below it, one Details panel, the same as the left column of the other record pages, holding Unit price, in JetBrains Mono like a deal's value, then Description, or "None" in muted ink. The lines from R6 sit at the foot of the panel in muted ink. There's no right-hand column, because there's no timeline, so the panel keeps the left column's width and the rest of the page stays empty.
